\subsection{Annotation and construction}
Each question records its atomic requirements (subject, aspect, constraints and typed expected value) and its evidence items with exact quotes and code-point offsets into the frozen text.
It also records one or more sufficient evidence bundles, the integration operation, a necessity audit (T2 and T3) or an absence audit (unanswerable questions), and its provenance.
Every pipeline and reader receives the same metadata, which is annotation-derived information that a deployed system would not have.

For development diagnostics, decisive spans were derived on the development set only.
A decisive span is the minimal sentence-level part of a gold quote that holds the value of the requirement that it supports, matched after Persian and Arabic letter and digit folding.
The development set has 158 spans over 54 answerable questions, with a median length of 113 characters.
Decisive-span retention is the fraction of spans that appear whole inside one item shown to the reader, for the best bundle.
It measures lexical containment and not entailment, but it behaves almost as a necessary condition.
Of 99 development cells without full retention, 5 were judged correct, against 79 of 115 with it (earlier run with a local reader).

The author designed the benchmark and its construction procedure.
Claude Opus 5.5 agents drafted the candidate questions, which people and programs then checked.
A curator agent chose information needs and wrote requirements, quotes and audits without seeing any method output.
A question-writer agent saw only that specification and wrote the question and the expected answer, about half in ordinary terms and half paraphrased.
The saved provenance gives the same agent identifier for both roles on all 156 questions, so the intended separation of roles cannot be shown.
% src: work/datasets_v2/questions/*_{dev,test}.jsonl provenance.curator == provenance.writer on 156/156 (checked 2026-10-06)
A mechanical verifier then checked the schema, that every quote equals the frozen text at its offsets, and that a bundle supports every requirement, and it flagged copied spans, answer leakage and near-duplicates.
An independent source-review agent checked answers, tiers (by the removal test) and necessity against the full pool.
The first-round outcomes (accept, fix, reject) over the 52 questions of each genre were 37, 11 and 4 for reports. They were 42, 8 and 2 for books and 43, 6 and 3 for laws.
Fixes were applied and replacements were re-reviewed.

A native Persian speaker reviewed the Persian wording, with 141 findings over the three Persian sets that were applied with a change log.

Finally, 32 of the 156 questions were spot-checked in an interface that showed the question, the expected answer, the requirements and the marked evidence.
Of these, 31 were accepted and 1 was fixed.
Every tier is half Persian and half English with an equal share per genre, but documents and tasks are not balanced, so language differences are descriptive.

Some report sources had been used in earlier, unrelated question sets, so for reports the test evaluates new questions but not unseen documents.

\label{app:implementation}

Table~\ref{tab:settings-full} collects the parameter values, model versions and providers, each fixed to one provider.
The pipelines are the author's designs, and Claude Opus 5.5 agents wrote and ran their code under his direction.
\input{tables/settings_full}

All pipelines share one chunking of each pool.
Sections are packed whole into chunks of at most 1,000 characters.
Longer sections are cut into windows that end at a paragraph, line, sentence or word break, with approximately 200 characters of overlap.
Chunks never cross documents, and the six pools give 58,218 chunks with a mean length of approximately 810 characters.
Every BM25 channel uses one tokenizer, and the dense and learned-sparse encoders receive the same normalised query.
Normalisation applies Unicode NFKC, maps Arabic letter variants to their Persian forms, removes tatweel, diacritics and bidirectional controls, converts Persian and Arabic-Indic digits to ASCII, and unifies zero-width joiners into the zero-width non-joiner (ZWNJ).
A word joined by a ZWNJ is indexed whole and as its parts, numbers are indexed as written and without separators, stop words are dropped, English tokens lose a plural \emph{s}, and Persian is not stemmed.
The indexed text of a chunk is its title and section path followed by the chunk, and the reader sees only the chunk.

Evidence is rendered with a header per item and packed up to $B$ characters.
\hybrid{}, \hr{} and \setr{} add chunks in order.
The first chunk that does not fit is clipped to the remaining space if at least 200 characters survive, and otherwise it is dropped and packing stops.
\lexb{} never clips and skips units that do not fit.
In the saved 6,000-character runs, the last item was clipped in 65, 69 and 62 of 96 packs of \hybrid{}, \hr{} and \setr{} and in none of the packs of \lexb{}.
The packs of \lexb{} were therefore slightly shorter, with a mean of 5,772 characters against 5,949 to 5,958.
The query embedding of \hybrid{} comes from bge-m3 served by Ollama, whereas the score fusion of \lexb{} uses the Hugging Face implementation of the same model.
% src: test_freeze/PROTOCOL.md sec. 7 (HYBRID Ollama dense vs. F2 HF implementation)

The four stages of \lexb{} run as five steps.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Candidates: one bge-m3 pass gives dense and learned-sparse vectors, and BM25, dense and sparse each return a list that is fused as in Eq.~\ref{eq:fuse};
  \item Evidence units: chunks become units, a sentence cut at a chunk edge is extended within a fixed allowance, and table captions and headers are attached, so that the fusion order of units, packed to $B$, is the fallback pack;
  \item Selector view and call: the top units are shown with a document legend, a header and an excerpt around the words of the question, and the model splits the question into parts and names for each the passages that together support it, joined by \texttt{+}, or \texttt{NONE}, and after the first bundle it may give one alternative after \texttt{|};
  \item Strict parsing: any grammar violation, deadline or error falls back to the pack without selection, and replies are never repaired, so that of the four rejected replies at 6,000 characters two listed three bundles for a part, as did both rejected replies at 15,500;
  \item Bundle packing: the packer enumerates every combination of one listed bundle or none per part, keeps those whose rendered cost is at most $B$, and solves Eq.~\ref{eq:pack}, after which the remaining listed bundles are added all-or-nothing, and finally fused units that fit whole.
\end{enumerate}
A part counts as covered by the prediction of the selector, which does not mean that the evidence suffices.
The pack without selection was run afterwards as \lexb{} without selection (\ref{app:posthoc}).

\setr{} follows SetR \citep{lee2025setr}.
One chain-of-thought call identifies the information requirements and selects a passage set, using the system message and selection prompt of the authors verbatim (Supplementary Material, Sect.~S3) over the top 20 chunks of \hybrid{}, with the GLM model as selector.
A cap of 1,500 characters per passage never binds.
The version differs from the published system in several ways.
The selector is an untuned general model and not a fine-tuned one, and the candidate list and the deadline are different. Furthermore, the selected chunks are packed first and then filled with the rest of the list of \hybrid{} up to $B$, so that every pipeline reads about the same amount of evidence.
A deadline or an unparsable reply falls back to the order of \hybrid{}.
All readers receive byte-identical prompts, which was checked by hash.
The answer prompt depends only on the annotated answer format, asks for the answer in the language of the question and uses \texttt{NOT\_FOUND} for abstention.
The selector calls of \lexb{} and \setr{} always use GLM-5.3-flash.

The reasons for three of the changes are as follows.
The token gate was relaxed because the full-passage selectors that were piloted at the time needed 5 to 7 thousand selector tokens per question, while the original gate left approximately 1.1 thousand.
A smaller excerpt-only order fixer later used 945 tokens, so the old gate did not exclude every selector based on a language model.
\setr{} was added because it led on the development set, and excluding a baseline adapted from published work after it had won would have been selective.
The judge was fixed to one model version at one provider because, on the development set, an unfixed judge scored one identical answer as correct in one run and as incorrect in another.

On the test, retrieval, reranking and selection ran once per question and cell with the primary reader.
The primary cells ran one process at a time, with the pipeline order rotated per question, while the other five readers answered the saved packs in parallel and judging ran as answers arrived.
An operational note added at the start of the run changed no method, variant, reader setting or gate.
A selector deadline is a completed execution and is never re-run.
Infrastructure failures were allowed one re-run, extended on 4 October to five tries for cells whose records were all HTTP 429 or local connection errors.
The log holds 123 re-run entries in total, 61 of them clean-up tries for one of the five other readers, and no cell that had produced an answer was re-run.
For the resource gate, calls with unknown usage are bounded per call and never counted as zero, and the comparison is with the concurrent \hybrid{} run at 6,000 characters over all 96 questions.
A malformed judge reply was retried up to three times with identical input, and if none parsed, the cell was marked as unresolved and enters totals and differences as bounds (0 and 1).
The first valid verdict for an answer is final.
The frozen report script excluded unresolved pairs from the sign test, zero-filled them in point estimates and bootstraps, and clustered by 47 source labels, which are finer than the 20 groups used here.
This paper instead reports ranges over unresolved cells, the sign-test $p$ range over every assignment, and clusters of questions that are connected through shared source families.

\section{Post hoc variants and analyses}
\label{app:posthoc}
% src: work/posthoc_2026-11/COMMON.md (rules shared by every stream) and S1..S15 RESULTS.md (what was run, exactly)
Everything in this appendix was run after the frozen test, with the test questions already used, so it is post hoc and exploratory.
The Supplementary Material, Sect.~S1 lists every variant and analysis, including those that the body does not discuss, with its cost, outcome and settings.
The same rules held for every run.
Prompts were fixed on the development set or before the run, and no setting was tuned on test questions.
Each variant ran once on the test set.
The reader and the first judge are those of the frozen test. The second judge of \ref{app:review} also scored 19 of the variants (Tables~\ref{tab:funnel}, \ref{tab:components}, \ref{tab:ablation} and~\ref{tab:search}). % src: S8_factorial/tables.md (7) and S16_judge2/tables.md (12) The ledgers of all calls are released with the code.

Several details matter when reading the results.
In the settings tables of the Supplementary Material (Sect.~S1), the gold-document and tier-routing rows use information that a deployed system does not have, and the BM25-ranked documents of the long-context variant were cut to fit the prompt, which dropped most of the gold evidence.
Only the two statute pools fit the 1,048,575-token context of the reader, so the whole-pool variant ran on three questions only.
In Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, selection over the candidates of the reranker scores the same as the pack of the reranker ($-1$ to $+1$, 9 wins and 8 losses).
An answer whose messages were byte-identical to those of a frozen pack reuses the frozen answer and verdict.
This holds for 37 of the 96 answers of \lexb{} without selection at 6,000 characters, exactly the questions on which the selection call changed nothing. Every difference between the two variants therefore comes from the 59 questions on which it changed the pack.
The agents were given 15,500 characters but used a median of only 1,652 (regular-expression agent) and 2,735 (BM25 agent), so they are not compared with single-pass pipelines at matched evidence.
Their billed cost per question (approximately \$0.001) is far below what their token counts suggest, because most of it is cached prompt tokens, so the tables report tokens.
On the 54 answerable development questions, \lexb{} without selection scores 29 against 37 for \lexb{} (+8 answers, 9 wins and 1 loss, sign $p=.021$, interval 3.7 to 25.9 points over questions).
Those questions were used to develop \lexb{}, so this is a consistency check and not independent evidence.

\subsection{Further analyses of the test answers}
\label{app:analysis}
% src: work/posthoc_2026-11/S7_analysis/RESULTS.md (all sections); every number below is in reach.json, contam.json, tokens.json, errors.json, channels.json or numbers.json
These analyses support Sect.~\ref{sec:components}.
They use only saved data, and no model was called.
Figure~\ref{fig:examples} shows two test cases.
In the first, the selection call finds a definition that the fusion order alone leaves out.
In the second, two judges disagree about a correct answer.
The English glosses are the author's.
\input{figures/examples}

For every answer, the exact message that the reader saw was parsed into its numbered passages.
A gold evidence item counts as reached if at least 90\% of its quote appears word for word, in pieces of at least 12 characters, inside passages of its own document.
A bundle is complete if all its items reached the reader.
A question is complete if any annotated bundle is complete, partial if at least one item of the best bundle reached the reader, and none otherwise.
The rule finds a complete bundle for all 90 gold-evidence packs and agrees with the identifiers of the annotation on 241 to 243 of 248 items for the six frozen configurations checked.
It is a text criterion, so it can miss paraphrased table evidence and does not credit evidence outside the annotated bundles, which is why 6 to 12 answers per variant are correct without a complete bundle (Fig.~\ref{fig:lost}).

\subsection{Statistical definitions and variance}
\label{app:variance}
% src: work/posthoc_2026-11/S1_evaluator/RESULTS.md, S2b_variance/RESULTS.md, S5_stats/RESULTS.md; formal definitions: paper/analysis/revision_stats.py and \ref{app:stats}
An unresolved judge answer is an answer for which the reply of the judge does not parse after three identical attempts.
A score is then a range whose lower end counts every unresolved answer as wrong and whose upper end counts every one as right.
All totals and paired differences are such ranges, and a sign-test $p$-value is the range over every assignment.
Groups are the 20 connected sets of questions that share a source document family, and the cluster bootstrap resamples them.
The mixed model is a logistic regression on every answer of all six readers, with pipeline and budget as fixed effects and a random intercept for the question and one for the pack. A pack is the pair of a question and a configuration of pipeline and budget. The model was fitted with a Laplace approximation in custom Python code and was cross-checked with statsmodels, as Sect.~\ref{sec:protocol} states.
The pack term is needed because all six readers answered byte-identical packs.
Its variance is clearly nonzero (likelihood-ratio $\chi^2=84.0$, $p\approx 2\times10^{-20}$), and a model with the question alone gives the same estimates with standard errors approximately 30\% smaller, which is the pack correlation being ignored.

Table~\ref{tab:variance} in the body gives the noise from re-running the reader and the selection call on identical inputs.
The standard deviation of a score is approximately two answers, and 8 to 15 of approximately 89 questions change verdict between runs.
Re-sending the selection prompt changes the pack for about half of the questions, but accuracy stays within reader noise (standard deviation 1.5 to 2.1 answers over the full pipeline).
The low score of 63 of \setr{} was a low run, since its two new runs score 68 to 69 and 68.
For a single paired difference, the reader alone has a standard deviation of 2.5 to 2.8 answers.
The difference between \lexb{} and \hybrid{} at 6,000 characters stays positive in all three runs (+5 to +7, +3 to +4, +4 to +5), is never significant, and has a three-run mean of +4.0 to +5.3 ($p\ge .23$).
The difference between \lexb{} and \hr{} stays at or below zero in all three.
Among the effects fixed before the test, only the budget gain of \hybrid{} is positive in all nine pairings of repeated runs (mean +6.7 to +7.3, 95\% group interval 2.0 to 14.4 points). \hr{} against \hybrid{}, a descriptive contrast, is also positive in all nine pairings (mean +6.3 to +8.0).
Because the two budgets of \lexb{} drew separate selector replies, one reply was also packed at both budgets, and the budget effect stays near zero ($-3$ to 0 and $-1$ to +1 against $-2$ to $-1$ for the frozen pair).
Separate selector replies therefore do not explain the missing budget gain.

Two re-sends of the selection prompt gave the same pack for only 61 of 96 questions, yet accuracy stayed within reader noise.
Without the 3-second deadline, 14 of the 15 timed-out selector calls returned a valid selection (median 2.99 seconds), and accuracy on those questions was 7 against 5 of 14.
With the smaller selector model, 40 of 96 selections fell back, 21 of them for invalid passage identifiers.
At 6,000 characters on the primary reader, equivalence of \lexb{} and \hr{} cannot be shown on the first judge even at a margin of 8 answers of 90 (90\% interval $-8.3$ to $+5.7$, TOST $p=.062$).
Under the merged scores it holds at 8 ($p=.017$) but not at 5 ($p=.092$), and the margins were chosen after the fact.
With 90 questions and the observed disagreement rate of 15 to 17\%, the paired design has 80\% power only for differences of approximately 10 to 11 answers, with 11.1 and 10.4 answers as the smallest detectable differences of the two primary contrasts, and 458 and 401 questions needed to detect a difference of 5.
Across the 48 per-reader tests of Table~\ref{tab:readers}, three have a raw $p<.05$ at every assignment of unresolved answers.
Within a column of six readers, only the budget effect of \hr{} for DeepSeek-V4-flash survives a Holm correction at every assignment (Holm $p$ .003 to .038), and nothing survives across all 48.
A random effect for the source group adds nothing once the question is in the model.
